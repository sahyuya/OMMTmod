Sound & Automation

Pick instruments and Minecraft sounds, then finish with volume, pan, tempo, and pseudo-release.

Instruments & Minecraft sounds

Normally, pick a note-block instrument — harp/piano, bass, bass drum, snare, and more — from the inspector's instrument list. Each entry also shows the block family behind the sound. On Minecraft 26.2, four copper trumpet variants (plain, exposed, weathered, oxidized) are available.

Choosing Other Minecraft sound reveals sound search, sound ID, and pattern fields.

  1. Type part of a name.
    Enter something like bell or ambient.
  2. Pick a sound.
    Choose the Minecraft sound to use.
  3. Switch patterns.
    When one ID holds several tones, pick a single tone via pattern.
  4. Press APPLY and listen.
Sounds not listed

New Minecraft sounds the Oyasai server cannot use yet may not appear as candidates.

NBS notes with no matching instrument fall back to harp (counts are shown on load). Zero-volume notes display only and stay silent in previews.

Automation panels

Below the piano roll sit four panels: VOLUME, PAN, TEMPO, and RELEASE. Volume shows first; switch with tabs. Each panel can be moved, resized, tabbed, and docked (see Settings → Layout).

Volume & pan

  • Volume: drag each note's bar or point up and down (0–100).
  • Pan: adjust left–right spread (−100 to +100, center is 0).

Dragging edits the selection relatively. MAX VOL at the top adjusts overall volume.

Tempo

Add tempo points at the playback position and set BPM plus curve per point. Multiple points change tempo mid-song. Curves are STEP, LINEAR, and SMOOTH.

  1. ADD AT PLAYHEAD.
    Place points where the change starts and where it lands.
  2. Set BPM and curve.
    Type each point's BPM and pick how points connect.
  3. Press APPLY.
  4. Listen.
    Check transitions are not too abrupt and speeds match your intent.

Select unneeded points and press DELETE (at least one point remains).

Example: slightly faster toward the chorus

Place points at the build-up and at the chorus, set 120 BPM then 128 BPM, and choose a smooth curve. Compare playhead and point positions while listening.

Pseudo-release on selected notes only

Pseudo-release retriggers long notes at a fixed interval, shaping decay or swell with a volume curve. It applies only to selected notes.

  1. Select target notes.
    Select only sustained tones such as strings.
  2. Open RELEASE and enable it.
    Check the selected-note count looks right.
  3. Set threshold and interval.
    Choose milliseconds or note values from 1/1 to 1/64. Intervals go down to 1/128.
  4. Shape the volume curve.
    Set start/end volume and curve; optionally add up to two middle points and drag them.
  5. Apply and listen.
    Check retriggers are not too dense and endings sound natural.
Safe way to start

Try one long note first with a modest interval and roughly 100% → 55% decay. Once it sounds right, add-select similar notes and apply.

Remove it with CLEAR SELECTED.
